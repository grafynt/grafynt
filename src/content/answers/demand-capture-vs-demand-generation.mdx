## The short version

- Demand generation: make people want something they were not looking for. Awareness, content marketing, most social advertising.
- Demand capture: be the business chosen by people already looking. Search, answer engines, local profiles, comparison questions.
- Capture is measurable now. You can run the questions your buyers ask and see exactly who is being named instead of you.
- Generation is slower, more expensive and harder to attribute, and it is the right job far less often than it is sold.
- For most small businesses, capture is both cheaper and unfinished, which makes it the correct first purchase.

## The real difference: who is already looking

Demand generation targets the uninterested. Its job is to create a want, which means paying for attention from people who were not going to buy today. That is legitimate work and it is genuinely hard. It is also slow, expensive per outcome, and difficult to attribute, because the gap between the impression and the purchase is long and crowded.

Demand capture targets the interested. The want already exists, and the only open question is who gets the business. That question has a definite answer you can look up this afternoon: ask the assistants and the search engines what your buyers ask, and write down which businesses are named. If it is not you, the demand is being captured by someone else, and the gap is the opportunity.

The asymmetry is the point. Generation asks you to fund the creation of intent. Capture asks you to stop losing intent you already have.

## Why capture comes first

| | Demand capture | Demand generation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who it reaches | People searching now | People not looking |
| Time to effect | Weeks | Months to years |
| Attribution | Clear, query by query | Diffuse |
| Cost per outcome | Lower | Higher |
| Measurable before you buy | Yes, by audit | No |
| Keeps working if you stop paying | Yes, organic positions persist | No |
| Right when | Demand exists and you are missing it | You are creating a category |

Capture is also the honest place to start because it can be measured before you spend anything. You can establish whether the problem exists, how big it is and who currently owns it. Demand generation cannot be tested that way, which is part of why it is easier to sell and harder to hold to account.

## The narrow-demographic part

Capture rewards precision in a way generation does not, and this is where most of the actual work happens.

Generation wants reach: more people, loosely defined. Capture wants the opposite. It wins specific questions asked by specific people, so the more exactly you can describe who buys from you, the better it performs. Which city or district they search from. Which language they type in, which for Indonesian buyers is often not the one the business writes its own marketing in. How they describe the problem, which is almost never how you describe the service. Whether they are comparing options or have already decided and are checking you out.

Each of those narrows the question set, and a narrow question set is winnable. Being named for everything is not a goal; being named for the six questions that precede a purchase is.

That is also why this site sells an audit before anything else. The audit is what produces that question list for a specific business, with the competitors currently winning each one.

## When capture is the wrong thing to buy

Do not buy capture if nobody is searching yet. If you are genuinely creating a category, the demand you want to capture does not exist, and the honest answer is that you need generation, which is not the work sold here. The audit will tell you which situation you are in, and it is the cheapest way to find out.

Do not buy it if you already win every buying-intent question in your market and the descriptions are accurate. It happens, usually where someone has been writing properly for years. Confirm it with the audit and spend the budget elsewhere.

Do not buy it if you cannot handle the enquiries you already get. Capture increases the number of people trying to reach you. Pointing more demand at an inbox nobody is answering makes the problem worse and costs more.

Do not expect capture to fix a weak offer. Being the business that gets named puts you in the conversation. It does not make a buyer choose you over a cheaper, closer or better-reviewed competitor once they are looking at both.
