## The short version

- Check it logged out, from the area you serve, using customer wording. Searching your own business name proves nothing.
- Cause one: the profile is unverified, suspended, or duplicated. Nothing else matters until this is clear.
- Cause two: the primary category is wrong. It behaves more like a keyword than a label.
- Cause three: the service area is drawn too wide, so relevance is spread too thin to win anywhere.
- Cause four: the profile and the site describe the same service in different words, so nothing corroborates anything.
- Only the first is binary. The other three are degrees, and they compound.

## Cause one: verification, suspension, duplicates

Start here because it is pass or fail.

An unverified profile is severely limited in what it can rank for. A suspended one is invisible, and suspensions are often silent from the owner's side: the commonest triggers are keywords stuffed into the business name, an address that does not match reality, or a service-area business publishing an address it should have hidden.

Duplicates are the quieter version. Two listings for one business split whatever signal each has earned, and the one you are maintaining may not be the one customers see. Search for your business name and address as a customer would and look for more than one pin.

## Cause two: the primary category

The primary category carries most of the weight, and it is closer to a keyword than a label.

The failure is choosing the category that sounds most impressive rather than the one matching what people type. A business that would be found by searching for a specific, humble service name picks a grander general category instead, and then does not appear for the specific one.

Secondary categories help, but they do not rescue a wrong primary. Pick the primary against observed customer wording, then add at most a few secondaries for things you genuinely offer. A category for something you do not deliver is a contradiction between your profile and your site.

## Cause three: the service area is too wide

Instinct says claim everywhere so you appear everywhere. It works in reverse.

A profile claiming an entire province is making a weaker claim about each place in it than a profile claiming one district. Relevance is finite and spreading it thinner does not create more. Worse, a wide area paired with no physical address gives the system very little to anchor to.

Set the area you actually serve and where customers actually come from. If that is one district, say one district.

## Cause four: your profile and your site disagree

The one most owners never check, because every surface looks fine on its own.

If your website calls a service one thing, your profile calls it something slightly different, and a directory listing calls it a third thing, there is no consistent fact about you to state. A human reads all three as the same service. A retrieval system sees three different service names attached to one business and no way to know which is authoritative, so it prefers a competitor whose surfaces agree with each other.

One service, one phrase, everywhere. The same applies to your business name, your address formatting, your hours and your service areas. This is unglamorous and it is usually where the problem is.

## How to tell which one is yours

| Symptom | Likely cause |
| --- | --- |
| Profile missing entirely when logged out | Verification or suspension |
| Two pins for one business | Duplicate listing |
| Found by name, never by service | Primary category |
| Appears far away but not nearby | Service area too wide |
| Appears, but details are wrong | Profile and site disagree |
| Ranks in one district only | Service area, or genuine competition |

## When this is a bad idea to chase

Do not chase map visibility if customers do not choose you locally. If nobody searches for what you sell with a place attached, the profile is housekeeping, not a channel, and the effort belongs on your own pages.

Do not fix this while your services and hours change weekly. Everything you reconcile has to stay reconciled, and constant change decays it faster than it earns anything.

Do not add keywords to the business name to accelerate it. The upside is temporary and the downside is losing the listing you depend on.

Do not treat it as finished after one pass. Google reshuffles categories, third parties suggest edits, and reviews keep arriving. An unmaintained profile is how a business ends up confidently described with last year's hours.
