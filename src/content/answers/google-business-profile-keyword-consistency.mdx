## The short version

- Retrieval systems trust facts that agree with themselves. Consistency is the signal; inconsistency is the absence of one.
- One service, one phrase, used identically on your website, your Google Business Profile and every third-party listing.
- Your profile is where local buying intent is highest and where the text is easiest for an assistant to retrieve, which makes it the highest-leverage surface most businesses neglect.
- The commonest cause of invisibility is not too little content. It is the same service described three ways.
- It needs maintaining, not fixing once. Profiles drift on their own.

## Why agreement is the whole mechanism

An assistant asked to recommend a business has to state something about you. Before it does, it is weighing whether what it found is reliable. The cheapest available reliability signal is whether the independent sources it retrieved say the same thing.

Three surfaces using one phrase for one service present a single fact, stated three times, from three places. That is corroboration, and it is safe to repeat.

Three surfaces using three phrases present no fact at all. There is a business that might do one of three related things, depending on which source you believe. Nothing there can be stated confidently, so nothing is, and the business that agreed with itself gets named.

This is why the problem is so often invisible to the owner. Every individual surface looks fine. The defect only exists in the comparison, and nobody ever looks at all three at once.

## What to make consistent, in order

**Service names.** Pick one phrase per service, the one your buyers actually use, and use it verbatim everywhere. Not a near-synonym on the profile and a more formal term on the site. The same string.

**Business name.** Exactly as it is, identically, with no added service words or locations. Keyword-stuffed names are against Google's guidelines, are a common reason for suspension, and are reportable by competitors.

**Address and unit formatting.** One canonical format. A unit number written two ways, or an abbreviated street type on one listing and spelled out on another, is enough to weaken matching.

**Hours and service areas.** These are the details assistants most often state wrongly, because they are the details most often stale somewhere. Reconcile every place they appear.

**Categories.** Primary and secondary categories chosen against the queries buyers use, not the ones that sound most prestigious. A category is closer to a keyword than most owners realise.

**Service and product entries.** Fill them out properly, in the same language as the rest. Empty entries are the most commonly wasted retrievable text a local business owns.

**Reviews and questions.** Both are retrievable text attached to your business that you do not fully control. You can influence it: answer questions in your own service language, and respond to reviews using the same phrases.

## Why this is sold monthly

A profile is not a document you finish. Google reshuffles categories and adds attributes. Third parties and competitors can suggest edits to your listing. Hours change for holidays. New reviews and questions arrive constantly, each one adding text in wording nobody has reconciled.

A one-off cleanup is measurably better for a while and then decays. ## When this is a bad idea to prioritise

Do not start here if you do not serve customers in a place. Consistency across listings matters most where local intent exists. A business with no geographic component has less to gain from profile work and should put the effort into answering questions on its own pages.

Do not do this while your services are still changing weekly. Every surface you reconcile has to stay reconciled, and if the underlying facts keep moving, the consistency decays faster than it earns anything. Settle the offer first.

Do not put service keywords into your business name to speed it up. The downside is suspension, the upside is temporary, and it is a bad trade on a surface you depend on.

Do not treat it as finished after the first pass. An unmaintained profile is how a business ends up confidently described with last year's hours.
