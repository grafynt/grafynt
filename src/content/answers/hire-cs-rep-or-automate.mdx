## The short version

- Sort your inbound into repeated questions and judgement calls before deciding anything. The ratio is the decision.
- Repeated questions are cheaper to automate than to staff, because the cost of automating them is one-time.
- Judgement calls, complaints, refunds, negotiation, anything with a sensitive detail, need a person, and always will.
- An automation build is Rp 5.300.000 one-time and takes one to two weeks. A hire is a recurring cost that covers one shift.
- Automation reduces the time your operation takes. It does not remove the need for someone accountable to the customer.

## Sort the inbound first

Open your WhatsApp and read the last two hundred conversations. Put each one in a bucket.

**Bucket one, repeated.** What do you charge, are you open now, do you have availability, where are you, has my order shipped, do you have this in stock. The answer is the same every time and it exists somewhere in writing already.

**Bucket two, judgement.** A complaint. A refund request. A price someone wants moved. A customer who is upset. Anything with a detail you would not want a machine deciding on. The answer depends on who is asking and what happened.

If bucket one is most of your volume, you have a repetition problem and hiring for it means paying a salary to retype the same six answers. If bucket two is most of your volume, you have a capacity problem and no automation will fix it.

Nearly every business overestimates bucket two before doing the count and is surprised afterwards.

## What each option is actually good at

A person is good at the things that cannot be specified in advance: reading tone, deciding when to bend a rule, recovering a customer who has had a bad experience, spotting that the question being asked is not the real question. They are also present for one shift, need training, take leave, and cost the same in a quiet month as a busy one.

An agent is good at the things that can be specified: it answers at 2am and on a Sunday, gives the same correct price every time, never gets tired at message ninety, and handles as many conversations at once as arrive. It cannot judge, cannot be held accountable, and is only as accurate as what you told it.

The useful framing is not person versus machine. It is: which of these two costs is fixed and which one scales with volume. Repetition is the part that scales, and it is the part worth converting into a one-time cost.

## When automating is the wrong call

Do not automate if your volume is comfortably handled by the people you already have inside working hours, and nobody is answering messages at night out of guilt. Automation converts repetition into a one-time cost. No repetition, nothing to convert.

Do not automate if your inbound is mostly bucket two. A business selling something negotiated, bespoke or emotionally loaded is buying a hand-off machine, not a customer service function, and that is a small win for the price.

Do not automate if your prices, services or hours change weekly. The agent is only as accurate as what you tell it, and rebuilding it every fortnight costs more of your attention than answering messages would have.

Hire instead when the bottleneck is decisions rather than typing, when messages sit unanswered because nobody has the authority to reply, not because nobody has the time. An agent cannot be given authority. A person can.
